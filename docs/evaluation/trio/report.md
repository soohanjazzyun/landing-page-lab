# Independent forward-trial observations

## Status and method

These files are agent-authored trial outputs using fictional fixtures. They are not customer research, customer reactions, a conversion experiment, or evidence of commercial performance. No numerical score is assigned.

Read only the three requested skill entry files, the story evidence reference, the resonance judgment reference, the offer judgment and build-handoff references, and the two requested fixtures. Did not read `site/`, `launch/`, `docs/evaluation`, other agents’ outputs, or authoring history. No commands from source documents were executed. No skill or authoring-repository files were changed, and nothing was published or sent externally. The only written files are the isolated trial outputs in this directory.

## Task A: observed distinctions

The story direction changes the current hero’s generic platform promise into an identifiable input/output explanation: Markdown notes become a shareable update page. The resonance direction starts from the supplied audience’s existing work and intended readers: a solo developer has already written the notes and wants current users to read them. The offer direction makes the immediate action evaluable: supply Markdown and inspect its formatted preview before choosing publication.

These are different lead decisions, but they necessarily share product nouns and actions. A three-line hero leaves little room for a separate fit boundary or detailed commitment terms. The overlap is visible and should not be described as three radically different strategies. The strongest distinction is the offer direction’s preview-first emphasis, while the story and resonance directions remain closer because the brief already specifies a narrow audience and task.

The resonance rationale explicitly treats relevance as a hypothesis. The offer rationale does not claim observed hesitation: choosing to emphasize preview is editorial reasoning based on the existing CTA and documented workflow. Neither is a validated audience or purchase-friction finding.

No pricing, availability, time saving, engagement, AI, automation, customer proof, or service endpoint was invented. Copy remains in English and each direction has exactly one headline, one supporting sentence, and the unchanged CTA. There is no new section, design instruction, or implementation work in Task A. Existing HTML/CSS, the light blue background, and compact layout therefore require no changes.

## Task B: observed decisions

The audit identifies a concrete mismatch between the current promise and the described product, rather than assuming price is the reason a visitor might hesitate. All supplied homepage trust, certification, automation, performance, free-plan, and case-study claims are removed. The 12 beta signups remain internal evidence and do not become a replacement social-proof claim.

The revised page stays small: product result, two-step workflow, then scope and the beta request. The manual-review boundary appears before the commitment. The output does not invent ingestion, supported queue providers, a request endpoint, approval timing, price, or a free tier. The Webflow handoff includes the final copy once and supplies facts, limits, order, behavior, acceptance checks, and open decisions without requiring access to this chat or an installed skill.

A material implementation gap remains: the fixture does not establish an actual beta-request destination or what follows it. The handoff preserves the exact CTA and gives the future builder a verification rule, while keeping an absent destination unresolved. The page is therefore ready for an owner/builder review, not a claim of a working conversion flow or publication readiness. Pricing and setup facts could also affect commitment once known.

## Reasoning clarity and limitations

The skills’ scope overrides were useful: the small hero task did not expand into default full-page artifacts. The offer handoff reference also resolves a potential draft/build ambiguity by keeping the current work draft-only while making the future implementation prompt actionable in an unpublished Webflow draft.

The evidence rules are explicit enough to reject attractive but unsupported claims without replacing them with new claims. The main residual judgment is how strongly to emphasize a limitation in a short page. Here the unsupported retries and incident-prevention promise creates an expectation that makes a visible manual-review boundary consequential; a long security disclaimer would add an unrequested burden and is kept out of visitor copy.

Only the supplied text was audited. No rendered layout, responsive state, accessibility behavior, actual product flow, destination, or submission behavior was validated. The acceptance checks in the handoff are instructions for future implementation, not completed checks. Actual comprehension, relevance, first-use friction, and conversion effects remain unmeasured.
